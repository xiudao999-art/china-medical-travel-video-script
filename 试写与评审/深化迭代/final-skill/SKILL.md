# English video scripts for medical travel in China

Create English content-marketing videos that answer a real concern, share a useful insight, or make a personally meaningful part of a medical trip to China tangible. The service role is a patient navigator and optional local companion. Clinicians make clinical decisions. Keep the visitor's choices and dignity at the center of the story.

## Read the right guidance

- For every new script or substantial rewrite, follow the end-to-end [production SOP](references/production-sop.md). It includes the brief, claim ledger, beat outline, draft, and release checks.
- For the course-derived reasoning behind trust, cross-cultural attention, and silence, read [story principles](references/story-principles.md). It also has a 45-second worked example.
- For spoken-English rewrites, scene choices, and format-specific examples, use [writing guidance](references/writing-guidance.md) when needed. Read the relevant sections rather than copying its example lines.
- When the user asks to test or improve this skill, follow [evaluation and revision](references/evaluation.md): preserve a baseline, use independent writing and review, revise the skill from evidence, and check a new topic.

## Core decisions

1. Choose one audience and one useful promise per video: a question answered, a difficulty made manageable, or an interest made possible. Select a form that fits the request; a direct explainer need not invent characters, and a city story need not start with a problem. Set duration and the desired viewer response. If the brief is thin, make reasonable, visible assumptions and proceed. Ask for a missing detail only when a material factual claim depends on it.
2. Build a claim ledger before drafting. Separate **confirmed**, **needs verification**, and **illustrative** details. Use only confirmed service and medical facts in publish-ready narration. Omit or explicitly flag the rest in production notes; never let a placeholder become a spoken promise.
3. Give the viewer a reason to keep watching and fulfill it. Connect a recognizable concern or personal interest to a visible action and a concrete benefit or discovery. If the story promises a check or an update, show what is checked or returned; saying “I follow through” is weaker evidence than following through in the scene.
4. Use the source courses as an internal lens: notice before interpreting, invite without pressure, add useful information or a small human response, respond to the visitor's actual answer, and allow space to think. A disclosed feeling can be acknowledged. Do not turn uncertainty about silence into a ban on emotion, personality, or warmth. SPARK may guide the exchange without becoming a spoken lesson or a mandatory five-part plot.
5. Protect role boundaries. An escort may help coordinate, navigate, and communicate within verified qualifications; they do not diagnose, recommend treatment, interpret clinical advice without confirmed qualifications, promise outcomes, or decide that an outing is medically suitable.

## Specific writing cautions

- **English:** Write for the ear, not for literal translation. Use short spoken units, concrete verbs, natural contractions when appropriate, and one thought per beat. Avoid “world-class,” “worry-free,” “seamless,” and other empty praise unless the brief supplies specific proof.
- **People and culture:** Never infer a visitor's needs from nationality or treat silence as agreement, anxiety, or rudeness. Ask about this person and give them room to answer. Do not frame the companion as a rescuer or the visitor as helpless.
- **Medical claims:** Do not invent doctor credentials, hospital partnerships, safety or success rates, prices, wait times, insurance coverage, visas, language services, or recovery timelines. Send clinical questions to the clinician or a qualified interpreter as appropriate.
- **Tourism:** Show the visitor's expressed interest and how the companion makes a local experience easier to enjoy. A pre-appointment day, a family member's free time, or another explicitly non-treatment context can stand on its own. Consider clinical activity limits when the scene actually concerns care or recovery. Do not promise suitability after treatment or make a tour compulsory. A hospital-focused video does not have to mention tourism.
- **Privacy and filming:** Use actors or consented participants; hide records and identifiers; plan around permissions for hospital interiors and staff. Mark dramatizations and testimonials accurately. No identifiable patient footage or quote without permission.
- **Marketing:** Lead with a useful insight, personal interest, or believable moment. Show what the help makes possible for this visitor. Choose one ending suited to the goal: a useful action, an easy relevant question, or an inquiry about a confirmed offer. Avoid both pressure and a vague “ask about our support” ending that could follow any video.

## Default deliverable and final check

For a request to “write a piece,” “write the copy,” or produce a spoken script, default to an English title and clean, directly speakable English copy, followed by brief Chinese notes when the user is speaking Chinese. Treat “article” as video copy when the established brief is a video; honor an explicit request for a long-form written article. Add a timed shot table when a production script is requested. Report counted words and an estimated duration with pause allowance; do not claim a real read-aloud timing unless performed. Keep verification notes outside the spoken copy and include only material outstanding items.

Before delivery, run the [SOP release check](references/production-sop.md#step-7-release-check): specific hook, visible service action, visitor agency, support for each promise, idiomatic English, realistic speaking time, filmable visuals, and one next step. Revise failed items before presenting the script.
